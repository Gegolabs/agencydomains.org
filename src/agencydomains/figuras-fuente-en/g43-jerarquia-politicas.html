Hierarchical policy composition · downward monotonicity

Figure G38

Example restriction: bank.transfer prohibited > $1,000,000
↓ inherits
2 · Area policy finance
Adds: bank.transfer requires approval > $100,000
↓ inherits
3 · Team policy treasury
Adds: trading only during business hours
↓ inherits
4 · Agent policy agent-treasury-001
Adds: specific tools · max amount per operation

Composition rule

Downward monotonicity The inheriting policy inherits the restrictions of the higher ones and may only add restrictions, never remove them. This prevents accidental privilege escalations.

Example case

✓Org prohibits transfers > $1M
✓Finance adds: approval > $100k
✓Treasury adds: business hours only
✓Agent adds: max $50k per operation
✗The agent CANNOT allow > $1M even if its policy tries
The agent's policy can be more restrictive than the organizational one · never more permissive.
The separation between declarative policy and imperative code allows changing policies without redeploy, versioning them independently, and auditing them without code review.
