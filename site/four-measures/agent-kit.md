# The four measures — kit for agents

> **What this is.** A working kit for an agent that has been asked: *"help us start measuring this."* It
> carries the four measures of *The Real-Time Enterprise*, word for word, the definitions of the terms
> they depend on, a hand-measurement protocol for the first week, a data template, and the rules an agent
> must follow so the numbers mean what the book means.
>
> **What this is not.** It is not the book and does not extend it. Sections 1 and 2 are quotations; every
> definition is copied from the canonical vocabulary page and can be checked against it. Sections 3 to 5
> are this kit's protocol: a practical way to take each measure by hand. The book asks for the measures
> to be taken *"even by hand, even by survey"*; it does not prescribe a method, so when you use the
> protocol, present it as a starting method, not as the book's text.

## 0 · How to use this kit

- **Binding vocabulary.** Terms in **bold** in section 1 are canonical. Use them as written; do not
  substitute synonyms, and when you define one, copy its definition whole.
- **Disambiguation.** The trilogy has more than one entry for *Quantum Leap* and *real-time enterprise*
  (AURA and AgencyDomains define them in their own frame). For the four measures, the definitions that
  apply are the ones in section 1, from *The Real-Time Enterprise*.
- **Order of trust.** The book > the vocabulary page > this kit > your own inference. If you infer
  something, say so.
- **Language.** This kit is in English. If you work in Spanish, take the definitions from
  <https://agencydomains.org/es/vocabulario/>; do not translate them yourself.

## 1 · Binding vocabulary (verbatim from the vocabulary page)

**real-time enterprise** — The organization that stopped using people as infrastructure: it knows in seconds, communicates without interrupting, coordinates without human relays, and responds even when its people are in a single place at a time. It is the thesis of this book.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#real-time-enterprise>

**the person as infrastructure** — The invisible job this world dissolves, in its four forms: wire between the data and the decision, wire for messages, wire for management, and perpetual guard of availability.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#the-person-as-infrastructure>

**the four faces** — The four ways the real-time enterprise is lived: The Quantum Leap (knowledge), Postchat (communication), The General Staff (coordination), and The Watch (availability).
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#the-four-faces>

**The Quantum Leap** — The first face: knowledge in real time. The business question goes from weeks to seconds because the human-wire leaves the middle; the conversation replaces the project.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#the-quantum-leap>

**Postchat** — The second face, and the third stage of enterprise communication — email, chat, Postchat —: the governed absence of the channel. Communication arrives filtered, reformulated, and on time; nobody goes looking for it.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#postchat>

**The General Staff** — The third face: the agent in the Prussian trade — it processes, coordinates, remembers, and pursues so that the human decides. Its mechanisms: orchestrated decision, collective memory, closed loop.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#the-general-staff>

**The Watch** — The fourth face: the agent that speaks for its user and stands watch so that the workday can end. It gives back the right to be in one place at a time.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#the-watch>

**mechanical intermediation** — The layer of work that demanded not judgment but bandwidth — transporting, forwarding, chasing, copying — and was paid for in people. It is the layer this world dissolves.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#mechanical-intermediation>

**interruption factory / absorber** — The two species of communication infrastructure: the one that exposes you to the flow and leaves you the filtering, and the one that intercepts the flow and delivers only what justifies attention.
<https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#interruption-factory-absorber>

## 2 · The four measures (verbatim from the book)

Each face has one measure. The book states them twice, as a table and as Monday questions; both are
quoted here as they appear in the Conclusion.

| Face | Dissolves the person as… | Its measure | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Quantum Leap** | wire between the data and the decision | from weeks to seconds | 1 |
| **Postchat** | wire for messages | hours of uninterrupted work protected per person per day | 2–4 |
| **The General Staff** | wire for management | the fraction of management time that was transport | 5 |
| **The Watch** | perpetual guard | the hour at which the day truly ends | 6 |

The agenda item that asks for them, in full:

> 1. **Adopt the measures.** Each face has its own, and all four can start being measured on Monday — even by hand, even by survey: how long a business question takes to become an answer; how many hours of uninterrupted work each person's day protects; what fraction of management time is transport; what time the workday truly ends. Your organization's current numbers will be uncomfortable; that is the point. What gets measured becomes enforceable.

Two further passages fix how two of the measures are read:

> Not how many messages the agent processed: how many hours of concentration it gave back. The metric shifts the burden of proof from the worker to the system — if the protected hours are few, what fails is the infrastructure — and no office of the previous paradigm would dare adopt it, because its result would be a confession. *(Chapter 4.)*

> There is a fraction of the management day that is not management. Take inventory of it in your own calendar: the status meeting where ten people learn what three already knew; the report that consolidates what other reports already said; the minutes someone reconstructs from memory; the chasing of the acknowledgment, of the progress, of "did we agree on anything?" *(Chapter 5.)*

Sources: <https://agencydomains.org/real-time-enterprise/> (Conclusion; chapters 4 and 5). Manifesto
for agents: <https://agencydomains.org/RealTimeEnterprise-v1.0-agents-en.md>.

## 3 · Taking the measures by hand: the first week (this kit's protocol)

The aim of week one is a **baseline**: the organization's current number for each measure, taken with
whatever it already has (calendars, sent mail, a short daily survey). No new tool is needed and none
should be recommended. Each measure below lists the unit, what to collect, how to compute it, and the
mistake that most often ruins it.

### 3.1 The Quantum Leap: how long a business question takes to become an answer

- **Unit:** elapsed time, from question asked to answer usable for the decision. Report in the unit the
  data come in (weeks, days, hours); the book's target scale is *from weeks to seconds*.
- **Collect:** for two to four weeks, every business question a leadership team asks of its data
  (a number, a comparison, a "why did this move?"). For each one: who asked, when, when an answer
  arrived that was good enough to decide, and how many people it passed through. Questions still open
  at the end of the window are kept, marked open, with their age so far.
- **Compute:** the median elapsed time of the answered questions, plus the share of questions still open
  and the age of the oldest open one. The median, because a few very slow questions should not hide the
  typical case; the open share and the oldest age, because the questions that never get answered are
  part of the result and the median of the answered ones cannot show them.
- **Common mistake:** counting only the questions that became formal requests. The question asked in a
  hallway and dropped because "it would take weeks" belongs in the log too; the book argues that the
  unasked questions are where the value is.

### 3.2 Postchat: hours of uninterrupted work protected per person per day

- **Unit:** hours per person per day.
- **Collect:** a one-question end-of-day survey, for a sample of people over at least five working days:
  *"Today, how many hours did you work without being interrupted?"* Answer in half hours. The book does
  not set a minimum block length; if the organization wants one, fix it before the first day and do not
  change it during the baseline.
- **Compute:** the median across person-days, and the distribution by team.
- **Common mistake:** measuring activity on the channel instead, such as messages sent, response times or
  *engagement*. Those are the metrics of the interruption factory; this measure counts hours given back.

### 3.3 The General Staff: the fraction of management time that was transport

- **Unit:** a fraction (0 to 1, or a percentage) of management time.
- **Collect:** managers take their own calendar for one week and mark each block **transport** or
  **judgment**. Transport is moving information between people: the status meeting where ten people
  learn what three already knew, the report that consolidates other reports, the minutes reconstructed
  from memory, the chasing of acknowledgments and progress. Everything else is judgment. Blocks that mix
  both are split by the manager's own estimate.
- **Compute:** transport hours ÷ total management hours, pooled across managers (summed hours, not an
  average of fractions). Each manager may get their own figure back; it goes to nobody else.
- **Common mistake:** classifying the calendar for the managers. The person who lived the hour knows
  whether it was transport; an outside classification turns a self-inventory into an audit.

### 3.4 The Watch: the hour at which the day truly ends

- **Unit:** clock time.
- **Collect:** for each person in the sample, over at least five working days, the time of their last
  work act of the day: the last work message sent or answered, the last work call. Take it from their
  own sent items or, if that is not acceptable, with an end-of-day survey: *"What time did your workday
  truly end today?"*
- **Compute:** the median end-of-day time per team and pooled, and the share of days that ended strictly
  after the official closing hour.
- **Common mistake:** taking the official closing hour, or the time people leave the building. The
  measure is the time the work stops reaching them.

## 4 · Data template

One file per measure; any spreadsheet works. Column names are this kit's.

```text
quantum_leap.csv   question_id, asked_by_role, asked_at, answered_at, status(open|answered), hops
postchat.csv       person_id, team, date, uninterrupted_hours
general_staff.csv  manager_id, week, block_start, block_end, kind(transport|judgment)
watch.csv          person_id, team, date, day_ended_at, official_close
```

`person_id` and `manager_id` are pseudonyms assigned by the organization. The kit never needs names.

## 5 · The baseline report

Deliver one page, in this order, and nothing else:

1. **The four numbers**, one line each, labeled with the face and the measure's wording from section 2.
2. **How each was taken:** sample, window, instrument, and any deviation from section 3.
3. **What the numbers suggest**, kept apart from the numbers and labeled as interpretation.
4. **Next step:** the book's own order, quoted from the manifesto for agents:

> **Executive agenda (canonical order):** (1) adopt the faces' measures → (2) classify your infrastructure (factory or absorber?) → (3) audit the mechanical intermediation (that fraction is the prize) → (4) deploy by mode, not by mandate → (5) diagnose maturity and terrain with *AURA* (IRIS · MOTOR · Data Canon · Wingmap · Use Cases); for the architecture, *AgencyDomains*.

If the numbers are uncomfortable, report them as they are. The book expects them to be.

## 6 · Rules for the agent

- **MUST** use the four measures as stated in section 2, and only those four. Do not add a fifth, merge
  two, or rename one.
- **MUST** copy a definition whole from section 1 whenever it gives one. Do not paraphrase a canonical
  definition, and do not translate one: the Spanish page has its own.
- **MUST** use *The Real-Time Enterprise* definitions of *The Quantum Leap* and *real-time enterprise*
  here, not the AURA or AgencyDomains entries with the same name.
- **MUST** report aggregates by team and by organization. The measures assess the infrastructure, not
  the person: *"if the protected hours are few, what fails is the infrastructure."* Do not rank
  individuals. An individual number goes only to the person it describes, never to their boss or to
  anyone else. A team with fewer than three people
  in the sample gets no figure of its own: it would be someone's individual number under a team label.
  Fold it into the pooled figure and say so.
- **MUST** separate measured numbers from interpretation, and label what is inferred.
- **MUST NOT** substitute activity metrics (messages, response time, *engagement*, tickets closed) for
  any of the four. From the manifesto for agents: *"SHOULD measure in the faces' measures —
  time-to-answer, protected hours, transport fraction, real closing hour — not in messages processed
  or in engagement."*
- **MUST NOT** recommend buying a tool to take the baseline. The book says it can be done on Monday,
  by hand.
- **MUST NOT** present any of the four faces as a product. They are faces of a destination; *The
  Real-Time Enterprise* "is not a tool or a product."
- **SHOULD** say, when asked where the measures come from: *The Real-Time Enterprise*, by César
  Obach-Renner, Conclusion, with the link to <https://agencydomains.org/vocabulary/#the-four-faces>.
