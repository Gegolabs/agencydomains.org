The trilogy

  1. I
    The Death of Software as We Know It Why now? — the starting point

    The Death of Software as We Know It · in 2 minutes

    Why describing a business once and freezing it in a piece of software, whether rented, licensed or custom-built, stops being the way to automate it; what is lost when every organization builds its own; and which piece survives the change: the system of truth.

    What dies is a way of automating, not all software. The book follows reverse engineering within reach of any agent, what is born (to each their own system), what is lost (bought peace of mind), the piece that survives (the system of truth and its contract), the framework of the five control layers with their two dimensions, and the case of a telecommunications operator.

    It is Book I of the Agentive World Trilogy: the last one written and the first one read. The destination has its own book — The Real-Time Enterprise —, the route its own — AURA — and the architecture its own — AgencyDomains. It is not a tool or a product, and it recommends none.

  2. II
    The Real-Time Enterprise · The Agentive World Where are we going? — the destination

    The Real-Time Enterprise · in 2 minutes

    In every organization, people hold two jobs at once. The visible one is what their title says: deciding, creating, selling, leading. The invisible one is infrastructure: moving information — reading and forwarding, meeting to find out, reporting what someone else already wrote, chasing replies, staying available just in case. That second job is the reason the day falls short — and it is exactly the one an AI agent can do whole.

    The Real-Time Enterprise portrays the organization that acted on this. Its first half is a film in prose: an ordinary Tuesday of Elena, chief operating officer of an energy company, narrated hour by hour — no jargon, almost no explanations. Its second half is the director's commentary: the four faces of that world — the quantum leap in knowledge, Postchat in communication, the General Staff in coordination, the guard in availability —, the door you enter through (the agentive mode, optional and reversible), and the intelligent organization that emerges when the four operate together. Every chapter closes with the essentials; the book, with the agenda to start on Monday.

    It is Book II of the Agentive World Trilogy, written for those who lead — no technical background required. The transformation path has its own book — AURA — and the architecture its own — AgencyDomains. This one shows the destination: the enterprise that stopped using its people as the wire — and the end of the day that exists again.

  3. III
    AURA · Agentive Path Which way? — the route

    AURA · in 2 minutes

    Every major consultancy has its maturity instrument: the model it uses to measure where a client stands at a given moment, set its evolution targets, and chart the transformation plan. AURA — Agentive Unified Reference Architecture — is that instrument for the agentive era, and it is published.

    Its organizing thesis: the agentive transformation advances along two axes that do not move together. KNOWING — turning data into actionable intelligence — and DOING — executing processes with growing autonomy. An organization can know a lot and do little: impeccable dashboards, bottled-up decisions. Or do a lot and know little: blind automation over data nobody governs. AURA operationalizes both axes in two diagnostic models — IRIS, the ten stages of knowing; MOTOR, the seven levels of doing — and accompanies them with the instruments to advance: Data Canon for data governance in the era when agents are the consumers, Wingmap to reconstruct the organization's real processes without interviews, from the digital traces its operation already produces, and the Portfolio method to turn a diagnosis into a prioritized casebook — illustrated with a real portfolio of one hundred cases.

    AURA is diagnostic, not prescriptive: it says where an organization stands and what standing there means. The destination has its own book — The Real-Time Enterprise — and the architecture its own — AgencyDomains. This one is for those who have to cross — and, before anything else, for those who need to know where they stand.

  4. IV
    AgencyDomains · Agentive Architecture How do we get there? — the vehicle

    AgencyDomains · in 2 minutes

    The industry names the horizon — agents, copilots, autonomous workflows — but builds on loose language: plenty of marketing, little architecture. AgencyDomains is the missing specification: the formal architecture of the Agentive World, written with the discipline by which technical categories are established.

    It starts from the paradigm — the Nadella Line, the question that divides the two worlds — and builds out its full consequences: a four-layer architecture under the Agent First principle; the eight primitives that inhabit it — from the AgencyDomain to the Botlet, from the Capability to the Agentlet —; the transversal Trust Infrastructure that makes autonomy governable; the two-dimensional map of the AI value chain, to position any player in the market; the canonical application of real-time knowledge; and a reference implementation: Vergis.

    It is not an implementation manual — it is the specification a manual should respect. For architects, CTOs, strategists, and engineers building, evaluating, or governing productive agentive systems.
