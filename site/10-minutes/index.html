The ladder · the 10-minute rung

The Agentive World Trilogy, in 10 minutes

The executive summary of the work — the thesis, the destination, the path, and the architecture.

Editions: I v1.0 · II v1.0 · III v1.0 · arriving cold? start with the 2 minutes

The trilogy holds a single thesis: office work is about to change shape, because AI agents can take over the part of the work that never should have been human. These lines summarize the whole work: the destination, the path, and the architecture.

Look at a leadership workday with an inventory in hand. A business question waits weeks because between the data and the decision there are people acting as the wire. An announcement gets forwarded and chased because between who knows and who needs to know there are people acting as the wire. Status meetings and relay reports exist because between the parts of the organization there are people — often the best paid — acting as the wire. And at eleven at night someone answers "just in case," because besides wire the office demands being the guard: on watch without a shift. Four roles, one invisible job: the person as infrastructure.

Hence the question that founds the work: if an AI agent can answer the business question in seconds, carry every message to whoever should receive it, coordinate and follow up without tiring, and speak for you when you are away — what exactly do we need people to be the infrastructure for? The organization that acts on the answer is the real-time enterprise.

The destination — The Real-Time Enterprise

Book I portrays that world, lived in four faces, each with its name and its measure. The quantum leap is the face of knowledge: today an executive's intuition waits four to twelve weeks for its report; on the other side it converses with an agent with governed access to the data and the answer arrives in seconds — conversation replaces the project, and when asking is free, the questions that never got asked get asked, which is where the insights were. Postchat is the face of communication: attention is a budget — three or four hours of deep work per day, at best; every interruption costs five hundred times its duration —; instead of the channel that exposes you to the flow, an absorber that intercepts and delivers only what justifies your attention. Silence changes nature: it comes to mean, with a system guarantee, there is nothing for you.

The General Staff is the face of coordination: the enormous fraction of leadership time that today is transport — status meetings, relay reports, the chasing of acknowledgments — dissolves through the orchestrated decision (everyone weighs in when they can actually think; anchoring bias vanishes), the collective memory (a graph of decisions and commitments), and the closed loop (nothing goes unanswered). The boundary is the design: it prepares but does not decide, remembers but does not sign. And the guard is the face of availability: a single agent that matures — assistant, mediator, representative — until it speaks for you, with explicit limits and traceability; the workday closes because the system stays on watch.

The first half of the book is a film: an ordinary Tuesday of Elena, chief operating officer, narrated hour by hour — the single briefing at 7:00, the assignment without opening a single application, the meeting that never happened, the dining hall agent-free by design, the close in the hands of someone who doesn't get tired. And none of this is imposed: the world deploys as the agentive mode — optional and reversible, like dark mode; the unit of adoption is a user, not the organization. When the four faces operate together, hierarchy — born as an information-routing protocol — keeps every layer of authority and replaces the whole protocol: authority, judgment, and signature made of carbon; transport made of silicon. People remain at the edge — which is where the action is.

The path — AURA

Every major consultancy has its maturity instrument: the model it uses to measure where a client stands, set its evolution targets, and chart the transformation plan. AURA is that instrument for the agentive era, and it is published. Its thesis: the transformation advances along two axes that do not move together — KNOWING (turning data into actionable intelligence) and DOING (executing processes with growing autonomy) — operationalized in two diagnostic models: IRIS, the ten stages of knowing; MOTOR, the seven levels of doing. Alongside them, the instruments to advance — Data Canon for data governance, Wingmap to reconstruct the organization's real processes without interviews, and the Portfolio method to turn a diagnosis into a prioritized casebook, illustrated with one hundred real cases. AURA is diagnostic, not prescriptive: it says where you stand and what standing there means — also for those not yet ready to cross.

The architecture — AgencyDomains

The industry names the horizon — agents, copilots, autonomous workflows — but builds on loose language. AgencyDomains is the missing specification: it starts from the Nadella Line — do you still open applications to do your work?, the question that divides the two worlds — and builds out its consequences: four layers under the Agent First principle, the eight primitives that inhabit them (from the AgencyDomain to the Botlet, from the Capability to the Agentlet), the Trust Infrastructure that makes autonomy governable, the map of the AI value chain, and a reference implementation: Vergis. It is not a manual — it is the specification a manual should respect.

What to do on Monday

The work comes down to an agenda. Adopt the four measures of Book I — all four can start being measured on Monday, even by hand; the current numbers will be uncomfortable, and that is the point. Classify each communication system: interruption factory or absorber? — copilots inside the factories is optimizing the wrong species. Audit how much mechanical intermediation you pay for: that fraction is your prize size. Deploy by mode, not by mandate: cultivate the desperate and let reality make the argument. And diagnose your maturity with AURA before promising dates.

The trilogy divides the work. The Real-Time Enterprise is for those who lead: it shows the destination and gives the agenda — no technical background required. AURA is for those who drive the transformation: the diagnosis and the route. AgencyDomains is for those who build: the formal architecture, primitive by primitive.
