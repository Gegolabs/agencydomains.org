The Agentive World in 2 minutes

One question, two worlds — and why it comes down to you.

2 minutes of reading · the next rung is 10 · the full book, about 3 hours

The question

Do you still open applications to do your work?

That question splits the era into two worlds, and the border has a name: the Nadella Line — after Satya Nadella, the first major industry executive to publicly bet that the answer is going to be no.

On the yes side, the Agentic World: artificial intelligence arrives as a copilot inside the usual tools. Everything gets faster; nothing gets different. Incremental evolution.

On the no side, the Agentive World: agents are the interface of work. You express intentions — “reply yes and confirm the date,” “how are this quarter’s sales doing?” — and the agent produces the artifact or the answer. Applications stop being where work happens.

What’s on the other side?

Three liberations, in order. You work without operating applications — your flow is protected. Communication comes to you, filtered and on time, instead of you living inside channels — your attention is protected: every interruption costs about five hundred times its duration, which is why the destination’s metric is hours of uninterrupted work protected per person per day. And in time your agent represents you, within limits you define — your time is protected.

None of this requires taking anything away from anyone: it is adopted as a personal mode, optional and reversible — like dark mode. The unit of adoption is not the organization: it’s you.

Why now?

The transition is crossed once per organization — and once per person. Eras are measured by the technology they coordinate work with, and the era that used people as transmission infrastructure has just ended.
