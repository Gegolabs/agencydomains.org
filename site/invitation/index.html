3 / 9

Now multiply it by your whole organization

Fifty people pay the toll of reading so that three can act. Status meetings, reports that merely relay what someone else already wrote, the chasing of acknowledgments — what fraction of your organization’s leadership time is pure information transport?

It is not a discipline problem. Work lives inside applications and channels, and between them the wire is people. Two thousand years of technology made the wire faster; none asked whether the wire had to be a person.

4 / 9

the pivot

What if your attention had a guardian?

There are two species of communication infrastructure — and they are nothing alike:

The interruption factoryIt exposes you to the flow

Filtering is your problem; its metric is engagement. The corporate channel is a factory by design — and a copilot inside the channel is the same factory, with help.

The absorberIt intercepts the flow

It delivers only what justifies your attention. Its metric: how many hours it kept anyone from touching you.

The fix is not a better channel: it is an agent between the world and you — your attention, protected by design.
